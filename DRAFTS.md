# Drafts — The Meeting That Should Have Been One Line

Judge (deadpan office satirist), criteria in order: (1) one line / one sentence
that could have replaced a pointless meeting, unmistakably office humor;
(2) kills the meeting outright — after this line nobody can justify holding it;
(3) relatable to anyone who has sat through a useless meeting, drawn from real
office life; (4) lands as a joke, with a turn or a sting, funny rather than
merely true; (5) stands alone, no context or explanation needed.
Steer: the line itself should BE the decision/outcome that makes the meeting
pointless. Avoid the generic "this could have been an email" — the field is
full of it.

## Draft 1 — the decision itself (131 chars) — REJECTED
> The budget is approved, the launch is Friday, and Priya owns it — this meeting is now just a reunion for people who already agreed.

- (1) One sentence, office humor, yes. (2) Contains the decisions, so the
  meeting is redundant — strong. (3) Relatable (budget/launch/owner). (4) The
  turn, "a reunion for people who already agreed", is decent but soft: a
  reunion sounds almost pleasant, and "already agreed" is vague about WHEN and
  HOW the decision was made, so the sting lands weakly. (5) Stands alone.
- Verdict: good kill, weak joke. The punchline does not picture anything.

## Draft 2 — the absurd artifact, the calendar invite (161 chars) — REJECTED
> The calendar invite for this meeting was sent to confirm a decision we made in the last meeting, which was called to confirm the email we all already replied to.

- (1) One sentence, office humor, yes. (2) WEAK: it describes a chain of
  pointless meetings but contains no decision itself, so it does not replace
  this meeting — it is a joke ABOUT meetings, not the line that replaces one.
  (3) Very relatable (invite → meeting → email loop). (4) The recursive chain
  is funny, but it is a familiar "meeting about a meeting" skeleton.
- Verdict: funny, but fails criterion (2), the second-most-important one.

## Draft 3 — the office object, the action items (115 chars) — REJECTED
> Our only action item from this meeting is to schedule another meeting to review the action items from this meeting.

- (1) One sentence, office humor, yes. (3) Extremely relatable — everyone has
  lived the action-item loop. (4) Sharp, self-eating sting. (2) WEAK: like
  Draft 2, it jokes about the meeting instead of delivering the decision that
  makes the meeting unnecessary; after this line, the meeting has still
  happened and nothing was decided.
- Verdict: best pure joke of the three, but it does not kill the meeting —
  it needs the meeting to exist to be funny.

## Draft 4 — the decision + the absurd image (135 chars) — PICKED
> We already approved the budget by email on Tuesday, so this meeting is just twelve people reading their inboxes out loud to each other.

- (1) One sentence, one line, unmistakably office humor. (2) KILLS it outright:
  the decision (budget approved) already happened, by email, on Tuesday —
  there is literally nothing left for the meeting to decide. (3) Deeply
  relatable: the email approval, the named weekday, the room full of people.
  (4) The turn is visual and deadpan — "twelve people reading their inboxes
  out loud to each other" pictures the meeting as a read-aloud session of
  something everyone already has, which is both absurd and exactly what such
  meetings feel like. (5) Stands alone; no setup, no names to know, no
  reference outside the sentence.
- Verdict: the only draft that is strong on (2) AND (4) at once — it IS the
  decision, and its sting is a concrete image rather than a vague summary.
  It also avoids the banned generic "this could have been an email" phrasing
  while using the email-approval fact as the weapon. Chosen as the final entry.
